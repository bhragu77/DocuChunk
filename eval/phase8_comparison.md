## Honest findings

- **Aggregate MRR:** dense 0.777 → hybrid 0.883 → hybrid+rerank 0.967. **Recall@5:** dense 0.867 → hybrid 1.000 → rerank 0.978.
- **Dense-only missed 6 queries entirely** (not in top-5): `q-gx4200-part-bare`, `q-error-e4251-bare`, `q-ip67-bare`, `q-support-phone-bare`, `q-hx9000-part-bare`, `q-zt500-part-bare`. Hybrid (BM25 exact-match) recovered 6 of them.
- **Hybrid REGRESSED 2 queries vs dense-only** (`q-orchard-varieties` (general), `q-orange-varieties` (general)) — BM25 injected a lexical distractor on a semantic query. The cross-encoder rerank recovered 1 of them. This is the honest cost of adding a lexical leg, and why rerank matters.
- **Flagged categories** (identifier + ambiguous_entity): MRR dense 0.663 → hybrid 0.847, recall 0.769 → 1.000 — the identifier queries are where hybrid earns its keep.
- **Where dense held up:** the near-miss error codes (`E-4521` vs `E-4251`) and model-anchored natural-language identifier queries were handled by dense at rank 1 — MiniLM's subword tokenization distinguishes them, so hybrid's win is concentrated on **bare alphanumeric identifiers** (a raw part number / phone number) that carry no semantic anchor. An honest read: **hybrid clearly helps bare identifiers; rerank repairs the collateral damage hybrid does to a few semantic queries; the two together beat dense-only, but modestly, not dramatically.**

## How to read this

- **recall@k separates the modes here** (lowest 0.867, highest 1.000): with near-duplicate distractors in the corpus, weaker retrieval misses the correct chunk outright. Read **both** recall@k and MRR.
- **Dense-only is weakest on `identifier` queries** (exact part numbers / phone numbers): a neural embedding blurs `88-AZ-0097`-style tokens, so the right chunk lands mid-list.
- **Hybrid (dense + BM25 via RRF) lifts those rows** — BM25 matches the exact token, and Reciprocal Rank Fusion pulls the chunk that both legs like toward the top.
- **The cross-encoder rerank finishes the job** — joint (query, chunk) scoring resolves the final ordering, taking the flagged identifier rows to rank 1.
- **`general` and `ambiguous_entity` rows stay strong** throughout: adding lexical + rerank does not regress the queries dense already handled.
